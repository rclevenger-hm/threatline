One context layer

Your tools know pieces of the story. Threatline connects the story.

Tickets tell you what was reported. Source control tells you what changed. Observability tells you what the system is doing. Meetings and chat tell you why humans made decisions. Threatline brings those pieces together around the work.

01

See what matters now

A Today view ranks the work, alerts and changes that deserve attention without replacing your existing systems of record.

02

Investigate with context

Open a work item and see the affected service, related alerts, recent changes, runbooks, meetings and decisions in one place.

03

Preserve what humans decided

Connect meeting and collaboration context so the reason behind a fix survives after the incident channel goes quiet.

04

Stay local-first

The workstation runtime starts on loopback, can operate without a hosted control plane, and is designed for explicit external writes.

05

Bring your tools

Provider boundaries let Jira, GitHub, GitLab, Grafana, Zabbix and future integrations translate into one normalized model.

06

Build durable handoffs

Turn operational actions, decisions and unresolved work into a handoff another engineer can understand without reconstructing your shift.

Privacy as architecture

Operational context is sensitive. Treat it that way.

Local-first by default

Threatline's current runtime binds to loopback and is designed to remain useful without uploading your operational context to a central SaaS service.

Explicit external actions

Reading and correlating context can be automatic. Mutating tickets, repositories, infrastructure or other external systems should require an intentional user action.
